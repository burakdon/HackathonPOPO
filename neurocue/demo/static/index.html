Who are you listening to

Two numbers make each patient: how severe the aphasia is (the WAB Aphasia Quotient, 0–100, higher = milder), and where the damage is. The six below span both. Pick one and the same pictures produce a different person, with their own words, their own errors and their own course of therapy.

What the two lesion weights are

In the Dell semantic-phonological model, naming is two steps: meaning activates a word, then the word activates its sounds. Each step runs over a connection weight. Healthy speakers have both at about 0.04. A weak semantic weight (s) lets neighbouring words win, so the patient says chair for bench or home for house. A weak phonological weight (p) lets the sounds come out wrong, so the patient says kwiyd for queen. The population prior that picks s and p was fitted to 74 real PSST participants; Dell's original prior is kept as an alternative.

How to read the heat map

Each cell is one possible patient: a semantic weight (left to right) and a phonological weight (bottom to top). Brightness is how likely that patient would have produced the response types heard so far. Healthy speakers sit at the top right, around 0.04 on both. Semantic errors pull the estimate left; nonwords pull it down. With 8 to 10 answers the bright region usually contains the truth; with 30 it is a small blob.

Two therapists treat the same patient

A course of therapy personalised to the patient: twenty words they struggle with, four sessions over ten days, scored the way a real study scored it, by what they can still name unaided a week and a month later. Both therapists run on identical copies of the patient with identical dice, so the difference is the therapy, and every line is the engine's own prediction.

What the trainer learned, on 80 held-out patients
  • Spaced retrieval with no cues: +0.32 retention at one week. The trained policy: +0.32, cueing on 1% of trials. Cueing hierarchy: +0.29. Errorless (whole word every time): +0.26. Massed practice: +0.23.
  • Why an override rarely lifts the curve. In the fitted learning layer a cue raises the chance of success now, but a cued success lays a weaker memory trace (0.79 against 1.00 for an unaided success; a failure followed by the word lays 0.59). The arithmetic favours a first-sound cue only while the word is still below about 40% retrievability, and retrying a word right after failing it also loses the spacing benefit. The trained policy already sits at this model's optimum, so a clinician's override is adopted faithfully and then priced: on severe patients it can edge the curve up, on moderate ones it costs a point or holds even. The trace weights for cued successes are assumptions, not fitted, so this is the engine's current opinion, not a verdict on cueing.
  • The policy rediscovered spaced, uncued retrieval practice, the best clinical practice, and found nothing better. A cue buys success now and a weaker memory trace later; that trade-off is the fitted learning layer's, not an assumption of the trainer.
  • One clinician override moves the policy in about 10 of 160 states, half of them the same kind of situation, none unrelated.

Where the physician comes in

The trained therapist is generic: one policy for every patient the engine can produce. A clinician is not. They know this patient: their frustration, the cue that works for them and the one that patronises them. DITTO is the mechanism that lets a clinician's corrections become the agent's rules: each correction is treated as preferred over what the agent would have done, the agent is retrained against its original self as an anchor, so it changes where the clinician meant and nowhere else. This step shows the loop, what it does over months of use, and the mechanism running live on the policy from step 4.

The correction loop
Every session, the agent proposes, the patient responds, and the clinician corrects what they disagree with. The corrections are folded into the policy, and the engine audits where the new rule applies before it is ever used on the patient. Press play to follow one correction round the loop.

The agent proposes a word and a cue. The clinician, watching, would have done something else and says so. DITTO treats the clinician's choice as preferred over the agent's and retrains the policy against its original self. The engine then checks every recorded moment: the rule now fires where the clinician meant it, and nowhere else.

What physician input does over months of use · an illustration
A generic policy starts out matching a given clinician's practice only partly. Each week the clinician corrects what they disagree with; each correction becomes a rule; the share of decisions they would have made themselves rises, and the number of corrections they need to make falls.
